# Scientific translation

Use this guidance for translation between languages or between technical, clinical, supervisory, and plain-language registers.

## Preserve meaning before style

Create a semantic lock list before rewriting. It should include every item whose alteration could change the conclusion:

- numbers, ranges, denominators, units, confidence intervals, and thresholds;
- increase/decrease direction and the reference or comparator;
- anatomy, modality, endpoint, population, and time horizon;
- observed versus predicted, baseline versus longitudinal, and direct versus indirect evidence;
- positive/negative findings, explicit negation, missingness, and exclusions;
- uncertainty, conditional language, study design, and evidence strength;
- citation-to-claim attachment and defined abbreviations.

Translate the locked meaning first. Then improve readability without changing it. Compare the final text against the source sentence by sentence for omissions, additions, reversals, and unjustified certainty.

## Scientific calibration

- Preserve `may`, `suggests`, `is associated with`, `predicts`, and `causes` as distinct claims.
- Do not translate “not statistically significant” as “no effect.”
- Do not translate “not assessed,” “not available,” or “not detected” as “normal” or “stable.”
- Preserve whether a value is measured, calculated, model-predicted, clinician-interpreted, or proposed.
- Keep drug names, model names, variables, equations, code identifiers, and validated scale names unchanged unless an accepted localized name is needed.
- On first use, pair a translated specialist term with its established English term when that improves precision.

## Translate for the audience

For a specialist audience, retain exact domain terms and follow each key result with its implication. For a multidisciplinary or supervisor audience, use this sequence:

1. What question is being answered?
2. What evidence is available?
3. What does it show?
4. What remains uncertain or alternative?
5. What decision, review, or next action is requested?

For a plain-language audience, use short sentences and explain absolute meaning before technical metrics. Do not remove clinically material limitations for simplicity.

When translating a table, keep row and column semantics stable. Shorten repeated wording only after confirming that each cell still identifies its source, endpoint, direction, and status. When translating a workflow, preserve every decision branch, stop condition, review route, and failure state.
